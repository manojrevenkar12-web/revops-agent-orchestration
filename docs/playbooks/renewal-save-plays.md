# Renewal save plays

Who uses this: Customer Success Managers and CS leadership. The renewal-risk automation cites these plays in its
briefs; a CSM decides which one to run.

## When a renewal counts as at risk

A renewal within 120 days is at risk when at least one strong signal or two moderate signals are present.

- Strong: product usage down more than 25 % over the last twelve weeks; the customer's champion has left the company;
  two or more severity-1 support tickets in 30 days.
- Moderate: health score down 12 points or more in twelve weeks; close date pushed twice or more; no customer activity
  logged for more than 21 days; the manager's forecast call is below the rep's call.

## Play: usage recovery

Use when usage is the main driver. Book a technical health check with the customer's platform team within ten
business days. Review which process models stopped running and why. Offer a solutions architect session on the two
processes with the largest drop. Track weekly active process instances until they return to the twelve-week average.

## Play: new champion

Use when the champion has left. Ask the account executive for an introduction to the economic buyer within five
business days. Run a value review with the new stakeholder: process volumes, hours saved, incidents avoided. Do not
discuss pricing before the value review.

## Play: support escalation

Use when severity-1 tickets drive the risk. Open an executive escalation with the support director, agree a written
remediation plan with dates, and share it with the customer within 48 hours. Hold the renewal conversation until the
plan has been delivered.

## Play: commercial reset

Use when the close date has been pushed repeatedly and the forecast is slipping. Align with the account executive on
the real decision date, the decision process and the paper process. If budget is the blocker, prepare a phased renewal
option with deal desk approval.

## What the CTA should contain

A CTA created by the automation names the driver, the play, the owner and a due date. The comments link the facts the
brief relied on (usage trend, health trend, tickets, forecast). A CSM can reject the CTA; a rejection is recorded and
used to tune the risk threshold.
